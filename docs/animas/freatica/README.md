## Identity

- **What it is**: Emilie's *why*, back and at work. Whoever wears it does not throw water at
  anyone: they **raise the water under the foes and hold it there**, in a cell with straight
  walls that nobody else can see the reason for, and everything that falls into that water
  (a spark, a flame) does more than it would have done alone. Her flat flooded because she could no
  longer join a cause to its effect; the ánima is that same water, understood.
- **Why it is not Tecnópata, and not Matemágico's water**: Tecnópata is a machine and clocks on
  one foe. Matemágico throws water: a status on a foe, twenty seconds. This is **the ground
  itself**: one body of water for the whole enemy side, with a level, that the wearer builds, holds
  and spends. Its intelligence is running a system, not a gadget.
- **What wearing it feels like**: being the one who set the board. You hit little; you decide how
  deep the water is, and what your partner's next spark or flame will mean.
- **Unlocked**: at the end of Emilie's mission, when El que no converge falls and the why comes
  back to her (`anima-emilie`, `winEmilie`). Shared: Gerard or Aitor equips it in an extra slot.
  Seven levels by EXP.
- **Name**: **Freática** (Aitor, 2026-11-20). The *nivel freático* is the water table: the level
  groundwater stands at under the ground. It is what the ánima raises.
- **Motto** (proposal): *"No echa agua. La sube."*
- **Colour**: **blue with green** (Aitor, 2026-11-20). Built as the water's own depth: thin water
  is green (`#27c2a0`, the first mark) and deep water is blue (`#1f5fd8`, the third), so the colour
  is also the reading of the level. The marks of the staff gauge are white. The one colour the
  interface and `CLASSES` need is a proposal (§ Open). Grey in the first round (§ Look and
  interface).

## Role

**The party's ground: it floods the foes' side, and the depth of the water is the strength of
everything that happens in it.**

| | Freática | Matemágico | Tecnópata | Papagato | Dadista | Sincopante | Prismático |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hurts with | Light water blows; the damage is in what the water does over time | Magic, with elements | Magic, clocks | Physical blows | Physical faces; the astral, by luck | The astral, through ecos | Light physical blows; what the paint becomes |
| Its resource | **The water's level on the foes' side**: a state of the field, not of anyone | Elements and their statuses on a foe | Clock statuses on a foe | Sangrado on the foe | Crítico (the Racha) | Ecos on its own side | Pintado on the foe |
| Pays off as | **Hundidos** at the third mark; **current** in the water from any electricity; **steam** that blinds them all from any fire; the whole of it thrown at once (*Golpe de ariete*) | A reaction between elements | Longer clocks, control | A golpe crítico on the wound | Críticos, Dados astrales | Ecos raised, Tutti | Estallido, Endurecido, Envenenado |

- **Good at**: several foes at once (the water is under all of them, for the price of one);
  long fights and big foes (a share of PV over time); making a partner's elements worth more
  (one spark charges the whole cell, one flame blinds them all); a cure for the whole party
  that costs water, not much maná.
- **Bad at**: a big blow right now (it needs three turns of water first); short fights; foes that
  resist water or electricity; being left alone (the water drains while its wearer does something
  else, and holding it at the third mark is nearly every turn). It has no physical blow and
  Fuerza −1. If its wearer falls, the cell breaks and the water wets **both sides**.
- **The decision it adds to every turn**: **raise, hold or spend?** One more mark, or keep the
  water where it is. Spend it as a cure, as steam against the blow that is coming, or all of it
  at once. With a partner it is said out loud: *do not light it yet, I want them sunk first*.

## Signature: La crecida

The loop:

1. **Raise.** A blow that says `Sube el agua` puts a mark of water under the foes. Three marks at
   most. With water standing, every foe is wet.
2. **Hold, and let things fall in.** The top mark drains in 20 s; any water blow on a foe starts
   that clock over. Electricity from anyone leaves **current** in the water; the deeper the water,
   the harder the current. At the third mark the foes are **Hundidos**.
3. **Spend.** A mark for a cure (*Bombeo*); a mark, to any fire, for steam that blinds them all;
   every mark at once in *Golpe de ariete*.

The rules themselves are shared and live in [`../README.md`](../README.md) § Agua contenida,
§ Corriente and § Hundido (the rule, and how it shows, beat by beat). In short:

| Mark | Seen | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I** | To the ankles | Every foe standing is **Mojado** for as long as the water stands, with no clock of its own. So fire does not catch on them (Vapor), and electricity runs through all of them (Conducción) |
| **II** | To the waist | The same, and what happens in the water is of level II: the current pulses twice as hard, the steam blinds at II |
| **III** | Over their heads | Level III for the current and the steam, and every foe is **Hundido**: every 2 s it loses 2 % of its PV |

- **The water's level is the level of what happens in it.** One number to read, the one the
  water itself shows.
- **Corriente** (Aitor's): any electric blow on a foe in the water is Conducción as always (it
  runs through every foe, Shock on a coin for the one struck), but **the water does not go**: it
  keeps the current for 10 s, pulsing on every foe every 2 s. A bolt while it runs only starts
  its 10 s over.
- **Vapor**: any fire on a foe in the water takes its top mark and leaves **every** foe Cegado at
  the level the water had (their blows at 70 / 50 / 30 %). At the third mark it is the strongest
  blind in the game, and it costs the sinking.
- **Agua contenida** (Aitor's, the mathematical part): the water stands in **a cell**, a straight
  prism with edges of white light on the foes' half of the floor and a staff gauge up one edge,
  its three marks numbered. It is a control volume: what goes in, what comes out, and the level
  between. The foes are inside it and the party never is.

**When nobody holds it** (proposals):

- **The wearer switches ánima**: the water stays and drains as before, and nobody can raise it.
  The passive *Nivel de base* stops holding. Any water blow from anyone still starts the clock
  over, and the current and the steam work the same. So flooding and then switching to another
  ánima to light it or charge it is a way to play, with the clock running.
- **The wearer falls**: the cell breaks. The water runs over the whole floor and is gone, and
  everyone standing **on both sides** is Mojado for 20 s, the ordinary status. Against foes that
  bring electricity, that is the danger of leaning on it.

**Where it shows**: in the scene, the cell and its water (the level is the clock: the surface is
seen sinking towards the mark below). On the wearer's plate, one continuous status icon, *Agua*: a
small gauge with three ticks, filled to the level, with a bolt and its ring while the current
runs. On each foe's plate, Mojado without a ring, and *Hundido* at the third mark.

**Its sentence in `ANIMA_RULES`** (the status screen's Ánimas page): *Crecida* · «El agua queda
contenida bajo los enemigos y sube hasta 3 marcas. Con agua están mojados. La electricidad deja
corriente en ella y el fuego la evapora y los ciega, más cuanto más alta. En la tercera marca se
hunden y pierden vida poco a poco. El agua baja sola con el tiempo.»

## Definitiva: Bajo cero

Aitor's, 2026-11-20 (it replaces that day's proposal, *Régimen permanente*, a level held for 30 s).
What is his is in the table at the top; the readings and every number here are proposals.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Wakes in a fight when** | The water stands at the third mark. That is Aitor's «un enemigo a full de agua» read on this design, where the water is one for every foe in the cell (§ Open) |
| **Then** | Once in that fight, for the turn and no maná |
| **In the game** | *Abstract*: `Congela a los enemigos que están en el agua, un turno por cada marca. Congelados no actúan y reciben poco daño. Romper el hielo a golpes les hace un daño muy alto.` |

**What it does.** The water turns to ice round every foe in it, each in its own block, as tall as
the water stood. The rule is shared, **Congelado**: [`../README.md`](../README.md) § Congelado.

1. **As many turns as marks**: cast at III it holds them three rounds, at II two, at I one. At
   the end of each round the ice comes down a mark; with the last it is gone.
2. **A frozen foe does not act** and **takes a quarter** of every blow.
3. **Each blow from outside cracks its ice**, a third of the way; a flurry cracks it once a
   strike. **The third breaks it**: the foe takes `30 × the marks its ice still has` as magic
   water damage (90 at III: *muy alto*; 60 at II; 30 at I), and is free and acts again.
4. **The ice is the water.** While it lasts there is no water in the cell: no Mojado, no
   Corriente, no Hundido, and nothing raises it. When the last block melts or breaks, the cell is
   empty. Bajo cero spends the water as *Golpe de ariete* does, for another pay: rounds without
   their turns, and one blow instead of three.

**The decision it adds.** To break or to wait. Breaking at once is the largest blow the ánima has,
but it takes three blows of the party's that land at a quarter, and it gives the foe its turns
back. Waiting is up to three rounds in which nothing hits the party, and an ice that is worth
less each round. With two foes frozen the party chooses whose ice to break and who stays in.

**Against the elements** (proposal): a flame does not crack the ice, it melts a mark off it at
once, with its steam (Cegado, as on the water); electricity does nothing to it.

### On the plates and over the field (proposal)

Following [`../README.md`](../README.md) § How an ánima is built: a fight's own mechanic is a quiet

**Built**: its look. The cell and each skill's look in the effects lab, and in the game the
ánima's id, its colours and pieces on whoever wears it and its interface (`ClassId`,
`ANIMA_NAMES`, `CLASS_STATS` as a first guess, `CLASSES`, `anima-looks.ts`, `style.css`), so the
fight tester can put it on. **No rule and no skill**: it is not unlocked by Emilie's mission yet
(`anima-emilie` still gives only her light, `ANIMAS.emilie`).

**What does not exist yet** and this design stands on:

- A **state of the field** in `BattleState` (the water: marks, the top mark's clock, the current
  and its clock), stepped by `runClock`, read by `react` (a foe in it counts as wet) and by the
  fight's summary.
- **Corriente** and **Hundido** as pulses on every foe, and Vapor's reach to all of them.
- **The cell** driven by the fight (its look exists, `createFreaticaFx`, and waits for the state
  above), the plate's icon, the pops.
- The bots of `just fight-sim` holding a level on purpose.

## For the balance, later

Nothing measured: there is nothing to play. To watch when there is:

- **Hundido is the one pulse in the game that is a pure share of PV** (Aitor's direction). Every
  other pulse counts PV only up to 100 (`pulseDamage`), for the reason in `docs/combat.md`: a pure
  share is the same answer to a boss as to a dog. At 2 % every 2 s a foe held under dies in 100 s
  whatever its PV. If bosses fall to it alone: 1 %, a share of the PV it has left, or a top.
- **Holding the third mark**: a mark's 20 s against a round's 10 to 20 s. With a quick wearer
  alternating Recarga and Defender it never drains; with a slow one it does. Whether that is the
  right tension, or too easy with a partner who hits with water for free.
- **Corriente at III on every foe** against Quemado III on one: its pulse is ×1, ×2, ×3 of
  Quemado's base, lower than fire's ×1, ×3, ×5 because it is on everyone.
- **Shock**: every foe in the water is wet, so a free spark has a coin for a lost turn. Held to
  one coin each time the water is charged, never while the current runs.
- **Cegado III on every foe** for one mark and a free flame; Cegado's 10 s may become 20 s
  ([`../README.md`](../README.md) § How long a clock status lasts).
- **Golpe de ariete**: the largest blow is three of base 26 on every foe, each its own golpe
  crítico roll; ×1.5 on a foe weak to water (Vertitrón).
- **Bajo cero at III on a boss**: three rounds without its turns is the strongest thing in the
  design. A boss may be held one round whatever the marks, or not at all with only its parts
  frozen.
- **Breaking it**: three blows at a quarter to land 90. Whether that is worth more than three
  blows at full and the foe frozen, by level and by party. A flurry (Papagato's claws) breaks it
  in one turn.
- **The largest blow**: 90 of base as magic water damage on each frozen foe, ×1.5 on one weak to
  water. It is not a blow of anyone's: never a golpe crítico.

## Open

- **Its one colour** (for `CLASSES` and the interface, which take a single one). Proposed: the
  green of the first mark, `#27c2a0`, with the blue as the deep end of the interface's ground. The
  deep blue sits near Prismático's proposed ultramarine (`#3d5fe0`); the green keeps them apart.
- **Grey in the first round**: built as grey edges and gauge over the same water. Or the water
  itself loses its colour until the second round.
- **Hundido's share**: of the PV the foe has in all (proposed, it can end a fight) or of what it
  has left (it never kills, a blow has to finish).
- **When the wearer falls, the water wets both sides** (proposed): the cost of leaning on it, or
  one rule too many.
- **«Un enemigo a full de agua»**: read here as the water at the third mark, since the water is
  one for every foe in the cell. If each foe should carry its own water, the whole of *La crecida*
  changes (a level a foe); recommended: keep it one.
- **A turn of ice** is a round here (the ice comes down when the round ends). Or each foe's own
  turn, which makes quick foes thaw sooner.
- **What breaks the ice**: three blows (proposed: it can be counted, and each is a crack on
  screen) or an amount of damage.
- **The ice spends the water** (proposed). Or the water is back at the mark the ice melted to.
