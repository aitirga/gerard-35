# Sagrada Família · the surroundings and the inside

Status (2026-10-31): **both are built.** The outside (`sagrada-familia`) is reachable from the
development picker and from the L5, with the basilica as a landmark module, the pond and its
reflection, the crowds, the Escoles' roof and the Falqués lamps. The inside
(`sagrada-familia-interior`) is a hand-built scene (`src/basilica-scenery.ts`) reached through the
Nativity portal and left the same way (see [Doors](#doors-between-the-two)).

## The real place in 2026

- **The Tower of Jesus Christ is finished outside.** Its cross was set on 20 February 2026, bringing
  the basilica to 172.5 m, the tallest church in the world (Ulm Minster is 161.5 m); Pope Leo XIV
  blessed it on 10 June 2026, the centenary of Gaudí's death. The cross is white, hollow and
  three-dimensional. Inside the tower, work runs to 2028.
- **14 of the 18 towers stand**: the four of the Nativity (98-107 m), the four of the Passion
  (107-112 m), the four Evangelists (about 135 m with their figures: winged bull, eagle, angel,
  lion), Mary's (138 m, crowned since 2021 by a 7 m twelve-pointed glass star lit at night) and
  Jesus'. The Glory's four towers are not built.
- **The Glory facade** (south-east, Carrer de Mallorca), the main entrance, is still a construction
  front: scaffolding, a hoarding, the bronze doors with the Lord's Prayer in Catalan (2008); its
  stair over Carrer de Mallorca is planned but not built. Two or three yellow tower cranes stay.
- **Two stones, two ages.** The Nativity (1893-1936, the facade Gaudí built) is Montjuïc sandstone,
  weathered ochre-brown with soot-grey hollows, sculpture that drips like wax, the green cypress
  with white doves over the central portal, four towers pierced by vertical slots and crowned by
  Venetian-glass mosaic mitres in red, gold and white, *Sanctus* spelled down them. Everything newer
  (nave walls, apse, the high towers) is pale cream-grey ashlar (since 2018 partly from Withnell
  quarry, Lancashire); the high towers are covered in a lattice of triangular openings. From far
  away the dark old front against the pale new mass is the read.
- **The Passion facade** (south-west, Carrer de Sardenya): bare pale stone, six inclined bone-like
  columns, a white pediment of bone columns with *IESUS NAZARENUS REX IUDAEORUM* in big letters and
  a cross, Subirachs' angular figures (carved from 1987).
- **Plaça de Gaudí**: a pond of about 48 × 55 m in green water with reeds and rocks, the Nativity
  reflected in it (the postcard), stone pines and palms, gravel paths, benches, a playground and
  courts at the Lepant end. **Carrer de la Marina** in front of the facade is pedestrian: queues,
  crowd barriers, the visitor entrance signs (A, B, C, D), tour groups behind a flag or umbrella.
- **Plaça de la Sagrada Família**: gardens with gravel paths, a playground and a dog run; cafés and
  souvenir shops on its corners.
- **Avinguda de Gaudí**: a pedestrian diagonal from the Marina/Provença corner to Sant Pau,
  pointing straight at the basilica; Pere Falqués' modernista lamp posts (the *Cinc d'Oros* model,
  set on the avenue in 1985 and usually taken for Gaudí's); café and ice-cream terraces.
- **Around**: Eixample blocks of six to nine storeys with chamfered corners; older ones in cream,
  sand and ochre render with iron balconies, newer 1960s-70s flats with continuous balconies and
  aluminium windows; ground floors of souvenir shops, cafés and fast food. Gaudí's one-storey
  **Escoles** (brick, wavy roof) stand on the Mallorca/Sardenya corner of the basilica's block.
- **The inside** (consecrated 2010): a Latin cross, five aisles in the nave and three in the
  transept, about 90 m long and 60 m across the transept; columns on a 7.5 m grid that branch like
  trees; vaults at 30 m (side aisles), 45 m (nave), 60 m (crossing) and 75 m (apse). The column
  stone follows the load: red porphyry (the four at the crossing, with the Evangelists' symbols in
  lit ovals at their knots), dark basalt, grey granite, sandstone. Joan Vila-Grau's stained glass is
  cool blue and green on the Nativity side (east: birth, morning) and warm red, orange and yellow on
  the Passion side (west: death, sunset), so the white stone and the floor are washed in both. Over
  the altar hangs a seven-sided canopy fringed with lamps, vine leaves, grapes and wheat, Christ
  crucified under it; the organ's pipes behind; gaps in the apse floor show the crypt where Gaudí is
  buried.
- **Crowds**: about five million visitors a year (2025).

How it stands apart from the other places: the Meridiana is an avenue of beige flats, plane trees
and red-white works barriers; Trinitat Vella is brick and render round a school; Cardedeu is low
terracotta; Alegia is green, wooden and Basque; Plaça d'Espanya (its own stream) is red brick and
fountains. Here a single building dominates every frame, the stone itself changes colour with its
age, and the water is a still, green park pond, not a fountain. The palace's pink never appears.

## Outside: the zone (`assets/geo/sagrada-familia/zone.json`)

## The landmark (`src/landmarks/sagrada-familia.ts`)

Built in code, recognisable from the diorama camera, not a sculpture-by-sculpture model. The pivot
is the crossing under the Tower of Jesus; +z the Nativity, +x the apse, −x the Glory, −z the Passion.

- **Heights**: real up to the nave's roof (40 m), the spires above it pressed to 70 % of their real
  rise, so the follow camera never clips the top of the Tower of Jesus when Gerard stands behind the
  basilica, and the overview frames the cross: Jesus 172.5 m → 133 m, Evangelists 135 m → 107 m,
  Nativity 98–107 m → 81–87 m. The ratios between the towers stay.
- **Towers from OSM**: the four of the Nativity and the four of the Passion as slotted parabolic
  shafts (a painted band of tall slots wrapping them) with mosaic mitres crowned by a white ball and
  a red cross tipped with white balls; the four Evangelists, Mary's and Jesus' as faceted cones in
  pale stone covered in a lattice of triangular openings (painted, 7 m a tile, small openings in
  wide ribs so they read pale from far away), with the Evangelists' white winged figures, Mary's
  twelve-pointed glass star (lit) and Jesus' white three-dimensional cross.
- **The Nativity**: one deep block of `pedra-naixement` (Poly Haven `rock_06`, 3.5 m a tile) with
  three pointed portals on three broad steps of newer stone, about eighty lumps of sculpture,
  sculpted columns between the doors, paler figures, stalactites under every arch, a ragged crest
  of small pinnacles and the green cypress with its white doves.
- **Nave and transept**: pale new stone (`carreu` tinted `#c9bfab`), five aisles with the central
  nave rising above, a gable over every 7.5 m bay crowned by a basket of fruit in mosaic, a rose
  window and two lancets under it, and the printed sheet of the facade to come on the works' fence.
- **The apse** (Gaudí's first work above the crypt, in `pedra-montjuic`, Poly Haven
  `old_sandstone_02`): a half ring of seven chapels between buttresses, each with two dark lancets and
  a slim pinnacle, smaller pinnacles between them; behind, the new apse in pale stone with six long
  clerestory windows, a ring of pinnacles tipped with mosaic and a cone of roof that Mary's tower
  stands in.
- **The Passion**: six leaning bone columns under a white pediment and its cross, the four slotted
  towers in paler stone (seen from the camera as towers only).
- **The Glory**: scaffolding sheets over the nave's end and two yellow tower cranes (alpha-tested
  lattice), their booms high above everything.
- **Materials**: 17 meshes, one per material (old stone, Montjuïc stone, new stone, the two bell
  tower paintings, the lattice, mosaic, white, red, dark, glass, green, star, crane lattice,
  counterweights, scaffolding, the printed sheet), all with the see-through cut round Gerard like a
  building.

## Inside: the scene (`sagrada-familia-interior`)

A plan of glyphs in `src/environments.ts` (`BASILICA_PLAN`, glyphs in `BASILICA_GLYPHS`) built by
its own scenery module, `src/basilica-scenery.ts`, like the palace's: a geo zone would bring a sun,
a sky and hills that an inside has no use for. The plan has the outside's scale (1.5 m a cell) and
frame, and the module builds in metres round the crossing at cell (36, 20), as the landmark does
outside (it reuses the landmark's mesh helpers).

- **Size**: 62 × 41 cells. Nave x −52.5…−15 m, five aisles over 45 m (walls at z ±22.5); transept
  x −15…15, z −30…30, three aisles; crossing 15 × 15 m under the Tower of Jesus; apse a half circle
  of 22.5 m round (15, 0). About 90 m long and 60 m across, as the real one.
- **Columns** on the 7.5 m grid, one solid cell each, by the load they carry: `P` porphyry at the
  crossing's corners, `B` basalt round it, `g` granite in the nave's central rows, `s` sandstone in
  the aisles, the transept's arms and the apse's horseshoe.
- **Walls**: the far ones stand tall with their glass, the near ones are cut low with a band of it,
  as in every interior: the Passion side (north here) and the Glory (west) tall; the Nativity side,
  the Passion arm's east wall and the apse low.
- **Doors**: `N` the Nativity (in and out), `S` the Passion and `G` the Glory, shut.
- **Inside**: `^` the presbytery two steps up with the altar `A`, the organ `O`; `c` the crypt's

### How it is built (`src/basilica-scenery.ts`)

- **Floor and plinth**: pale marble slabs (`marbre`, tinted `#ddd7cb`, a little gloss) over every
  cell inside the walls, on a dark plinth shaped like the plan, so the model floats over the dark
  backdrop like the palace.
- **The forest**: each column a trunk whose section is a star of twelve, ten, eight or six points
  (porphyry, basalt, granite, sandstone, after the basilica's own account), turning as it rises with
  its flutes fading towards the knot; the knot; the trunk going on up to the vault; three or four
  branches leaving the knot, each forking once. Polished stone with painted grain, branches in bone
  white. The four porphyry knots carry the Evangelists' lit ovals facing the camera (Mark and Luke
  towards the apse, as in the 2026 photos; John and Matthew towards the nave).
- **The vault**: over each column a cell, a shallow eight-sided funnel opening upwards, painted as
  the vault looks from below (white stone, gold-peach rays round the oculus, small lit openings),
  tinted warm on the Passion half and cool on the Nativity half. They overlap, and step up from the
  aisles (10.5 m) to the nave and transept (13.5 m), the apse (15.5 m) and the crossing (17 m),
  the real 30, 45, 75 and 60 m pressed so the canopy sits in the frame. The crossing has none: the
  lantern's gold light lies on the floor there.
- **The clearing**: the vault's material has its own shader chunk (GLSL and WGSL) that opens a
  round clearing along the line from Gerard to the camera (8 world units, a warm glowing edge), like
  the buildings' cut outside; and as the camera pulls back to the overview, a plane sweeps in to the
  crossing and opens the vault's near half, so the overview reads as a model cut in two: the vault
  over the far half, the forest of trunks and branches and the coloured floor in the near one.
- **Glass**: Vila-Grau's two palettes painted as leaded panes (own procedural painting), glowing:
  warm (reds, oranges, yellows) on the Passion side's tall walls (a pair of lancets and a rose in each
  nave bay, three tall windows and a rose over the Passion's bronze door at the arm's end), cool
  (blues, greens) on the Nativity side's low walls, gold round the apse. The Glory's wall carries the
  multicoloured great rose, two smaller roses, four lancets over the choir's gallery, a blue window
  over each side door and the bronze doors with the Lord's Prayer in Catalan (*Doneu-nos avui el pa
  de cada dia* in gold).
- **The light**: pools of light on the floor, each the glass's own painting blurred and faded at its
  edges, cool on the Nativity half and warm on the Passion half, stretched along the sun's path; they
  slide slowly together and breathe (the sun walking). Coloured lamps by each side tint the stone,
  the canopy's lamps and a gold one in the apse warm the presbytery, a low sun throws the trunks'
  shadows across the floor, and motes hang in the air.
- **The presbytery**: two steps of white stone behind a red rope on brass posts, the guard at its
  head; the porphyry altar with its cloth; the seven-sided canopy (cream parasol, gilt rim, fifty
  lamps, vine leaves, grapes and ears of wheat) with Christ on the cross under it, hung by four cords;
  two banks of organ pipes behind.
- **Also**: the crypt's two grilles in the ambulatory (a dark opening with warm light below and a
  brass rail), rows of wooden chairs in the nave, visitors from the outside's crowd module (looking
  at the glass of their side, the altar from the crossing, some holding a phone up), the sign by the
  door (*SILENCI · SILENCIO · SILENCE*), red ropes across the shut doors.
- **Cost**: about 410–460 draw calls in the debug build (1440 × 900), 16.7 ms a frame at the 60 fps
  cap, on WebGPU and WebGL2.

## Approximate and open

- Not walked in person: the reference is OSM plus 2026 Commons photos; the ground-floor shops come
  from OSM and may lag. The flats round the block keep the style default (seven storeys).
- The basilica is a massing with signature details, not a survey: the spires above the nave are
  pressed to 70 % of their rise; the Passion's sculpture, the Sanctus letters, the Evangelists'
  exact figures and the nave's windows are suggested, not modelled; up close (the story's camera)
  the lattice and the mitres read as paintings.
- The pond's reflection is painted in the camera's frame, not mirrored: it shows the spires, but a
  real mirror from this camera would show the trees on the far shore.
- The Passion facade faces away from the camera: only its towers and the visitors in front of it
  show. Its magic square (every line adds to 33) is for the story.
- Inside, heights are pressed (knots at 6.5–10 m, the vault at 10.5–17 m instead of 30–75 m), and
  the vault is a set of overlapping cells over the columns, not the real continuous hyperboloid
  surface; it is painted as seen from below although the camera sees it from above. Columns fork
  once (the real ones keep branching); the sandstone apse colonnade is a horseshoe of four, not the
  real ring. The canopy is cream, not the real white with lettering; Christ is a simple figure.
- The light pools are painted, not cast: they drift as one and do not follow the windows exactly.
  The low sun lights the floor through the vault (it casts no shadow), which a real nave would not.
- The Nativity portal opens for anyone; the mission that uses the inside will decide whether the
  guard's lines come back as a gate (`story.gate`).
- No sound of its own yet (the hush, an organ note): `PLACE_SONGS` has nothing for either place.
- The totem shows L5 only; the real station is also L2, which the network map does not have yet.
- Until the Meridiana stream's city edge is merged, wooded hills close the diorama's far sides,
  which a city centre would not have.
- The park's trees cost shadows: by the pond the frame draws about 2 500 calls in the debug build,
  a little over the Meridiana's 2 270 (the forward pass is under it).
- Which mission uses these places, and whether the Passion door ever opens, is for the story.
